## Learning guide
**The arc**
The starting problem is spending time with AI without improving how work is planned, reviewed, or handed off. Claude artifacts turn information into an interactive working surface, not just a finished response. The guide moves from mapping time and reviewing plans to collaboration, narrowly scoped tools, and redesigned workflows that can become reusable skills.

**1. Map time before building improvements**
- **Visualize your AI work:** Ask Claude to examine past sessions, show where time goes, and suggest skills to create or improve. Use a pie chart to identify the activities consuming the most time and focus improvement efforts there.
- **Approve recommendations selectively:** Respond to individual recommendations with inline comments indicating what to do or reject, then send those comments back to Claude to revise the artifact. This makes the plan reflect your decisions rather than treating every generated suggestion as a requirement.
- **Build only after review:** Once the plan is satisfactory, return to the original chat and ask Claude to create only the approved skills. Follow planning, review, revision, then implementation for complex automations, document processing, and new product features too.

**2. Keep collaboration inside the working document**
- **Request a document artifact:** Use the phrase “artifact document” when asking Claude for writing that benefits from review, such as marketing materials, LinkedIn posts, or short-form scripts. Edit directly inline, or highlight text to ask a question or request a targeted adjustment, so drafting and AI assistance stay together.
- **Structure the collaboration:** Separate **ask**, **review**, **handoff**, and **execution**: specify the request, assess whether it is appropriate and feasible, confirm the agreed work, then do it. Package notes into an artifact and have the reviewer respond there, rather than using AI only for the final execution step.
- **Consolidate QA decisions:** Put testing notes, expected behavior, actual findings, and requested changes in one artifact. Review each item as an accepted change, rejected request, or intended feature rather than a bug; then ask Claude to apply the approved feedback.
- **Read before sharing:** Read the entire generated artifact before sending it to a teammate or client. Polished presentation can disguise weak content, and forwarding unread output makes the recipient do the evaluation you should have done.

**3. Build microtools for specific recurring problems**
- **Choose one small job:** Define a microtool as a tiny piece of software with one specific purpose, especially a task too small to justify a conventional build. For a client with five restaurants, the example was a cocktail reference searchable by drink name or spirit, with preparation instructions accessible through a phone link without a login or separate app.
- **Audit for neglected needs:** Ask Claude to examine your setup and identify work that could benefit from a microtool. Look for useful tasks that remain unprioritized, rather than expanding the idea into a broad application.
- **Centralize feature requests:** If requests are scattered across calls, app feedback, and mental notes, ask for a feature-request triage board. The example board compiled calls and Slack messages into a work list with request statuses and a refresh button, using available connectors to pull in Slack content and call transcripts.
- **Serve narrow customer needs:** Build external microtools for specific customer problems, even when only one or two customers need them. Use Share in the top-right corner to make the artifact publicly accessible and give customers a link to the tool.
- **Make lead magnets genuinely useful:** Choose a free tool that delivers real value while requiring relatively little work to create, especially when audience demand is uncertain. Ask Claude to suggest customer-facing microtools from your work, then share a useful tool through a direct message or email as an offering exchanged for contact information.

**4. Change the handoff, not just the tool**
- **Find visual gaps:** Audit your workday for places where a visual component would communicate intent better than text alone. In the Instagram-graphics example, a text list in Notion left a gap between the requested content and the finished design, creating repeated exchanges with the designer.
- **Show the intended layout:** Create an artifact that positions text where it should appear on the eventual Instagram graphic, then share it as the design handoff. Use it to clarify the general layout, not to replace the designer or the finished graphic.
- **Prototype before standardizing:** Ask Claude to build a proof-of-concept artifact and iterate until its style and format meet your needs. This lets you resolve the handoff format before making it a repeatable workflow.
- **Capture the successful format:** Once the artifact works, ask Claude to turn it into a skill that preserves the preferred output format. The Instagram example used a skill named **IG graphic creation** so future artifacts followed the refined template.
- **Make abilities visible:** For hiring, ask candidates to share an artifact using the company's branding to visualize their abilities. As a job seeker, link a personal-profile artifact from a résumé or LinkedIn profile to demonstrate an AI-native approach.

**5. Make artifacts a deliberate workflow choice**
- **Reconsider routine documents:** When writing a document or explaining something, ask whether the work should be AI-enabled and whether an artifact is an appropriate format. Start with planning, where a visual, reviewable artifact can clarify decisions before implementation begins.

**Try this first:** Ask Claude to examine your past sessions and create an artifact with a pie chart of where your AI work time goes plus suggested skills to improve; review those suggestions before approving any build.
**Caveat:** The artifact capabilities and connector-based refresh behavior described here are not independently verified in the notes, and the promoted output multipliers are not measured results.
