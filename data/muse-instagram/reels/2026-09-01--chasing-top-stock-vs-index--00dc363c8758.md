## Derived summary
Ricky, a creator who runs an investing series for parents, presents a backtest asking what happens when you chase the hottest stock in the S&P 500 every year instead of buying the index. Starting in 2006, he invested $1,000 each year in the previous year's best-performing S&P 500 company and compared it with $1,000 in the S&P 500. His headline examples: Apple's 2005 win grew $1,000 to $141,700 versus $8,000 in the index; Nvidia's 2017 win grew to $88,000 versus $3,600; Netflix's 2013 win to $11,600 versus $3,100; AMD's selections grew to $25,000 and $9,600 versus $3,600; Tesla's 2020 gain of 743% grew to $2,300 versus $1,900. In total, the prior-year-winners strategy turned $20,000 invested over the years into $357,000, while the index turned the same into $105,000, a difference of roughly $250,000. He notes the outperformance was heavily concentrated in just a few stocks, mainly Apple and Nvidia. His conclusion: momentum can continue, sometimes, but one year's return alone does not tell you what happens next, and past performance does not guarantee future results. All figures are the creator's self-reported backtest, not verified financial analysis.

## Practical takeaways
- A momentum strategy can backtest well when a few mega-winners dominate; concentration, not the strategy's reliability, drove this result.
- Never read a backtest as a forward promise: the reel's own conclusion is that a single year's return says little about what comes next.
- When evaluating any strategy, ask how much of the outperformance came from a handful of picks, and what the portfolio did in the other years.
