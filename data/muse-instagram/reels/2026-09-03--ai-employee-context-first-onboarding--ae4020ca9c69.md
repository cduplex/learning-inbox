## Derived summary
Kiara, founder of "Ai for Business," argues that people overcomplicate AI use by jumping to complex agents, and instead should treat their AI like a new employee. Using a small whiteboard, she writes out an onboarding framework: who you are, what your business is, how you work, current projects, and rules. Her core point is that AI needs this business context first, exactly like onboarding a human hire, before it is given SOPs or skills, which she defines as simple instructions for repeatable tasks ("how to do this job"). Her other core message: the user must remain the thinker and not delegate thinking to AI, in order to avoid "AI slop" and get useful work. The tone is direct and aimed at non-technical business owners; comments are supportive, with viewers praising the clarity and several saying they had built their own systems on similar lines by accident. This is practitioner advice from one creator, presented as a framework rather than tested research.

## Practical takeaways
- Write a short context document (identity, business, working style, projects, rules) and give it to the model before any task instructions.
- Define skills as simple repeatable-task instructions, and only layer them on after the context is in place.
- Keep yourself as the thinker in the loop: delegate execution, not judgment, to avoid generic low-quality output.
- Resist building complex agents until the basic context-plus-instructions setup is working reliably.
