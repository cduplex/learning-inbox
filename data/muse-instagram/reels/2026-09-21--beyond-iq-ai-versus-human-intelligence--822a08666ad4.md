## Derived summary
Celastrina opens the second season of her "Beyond IQ" series with an argument that the important question is not whether to use AI, but what kind of relationship with it we build. Drawing on the brain's "use it or lose it" principle, she points to GPS reducing spatial navigation skills and to London taxi drivers' hippocampi shrinking after GPS became widespread, as a warning about skills that fade when they are delegated. She argues we have also lost the ability to sit with a question long enough for complex thought, which weakens decision making and tolerance for uncertainty. This is not framed as an anti-AI position: the proposed alternative is to use AI as a thinking partner that pushes back and stress tests ideas, so the user remains the one thinking. She closes by saying AI cannot replicate consciousness, presence, genuine empathy, or the judgment about what should never be optimized, capacities she roots in lived experience, fear, love, and consequence, and presents raising human consciousness about what intelligence truly is as the urgent project of the next decade. The specific neuroscience claims are presented as the creator's framing and should be checked against primary sources before being repeated as fact.

## Practical takeaways
- Before delegating a task to AI, ask whether the task is one whose skill you want to keep: delegate the chore, practice the judgment.
- Use AI as a sparring partner: ask it to challenge your reasoning and surface counterarguments, not just to produce an answer.
- Build in a pause before accepting AI output on consequential decisions, and write down your own answer first when the decision matters.
- Protect the skills that compound: navigation, memory, and sustained attention degrade quietly, so exercise them deliberately.
- Decide in advance what should never be delegated, such as judgments rooted in empathy, values, or lived experience, and keep those decisions human.
