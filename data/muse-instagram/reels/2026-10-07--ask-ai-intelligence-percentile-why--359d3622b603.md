## Core components
- A two-part self-reflection prompt for the AI you use most
- First ask the AI to estimate your intelligence percentile based on past interactions and training data
- Then ask it to explain why, with specifics on reasoning style, abstraction, weaknesses, and growth areas
- The explanation, not the number, is the useful output

## Derived summary
Nate B. Jones shares a prompt he calls a fun experiment: ask your most-used AI to estimate your intelligence percentile, then ask it to tell you why. In his own try, the numeric score mattered less than the detailed feedback that followed, which described his reasoning style, how he handles abstraction, his weaknesses, and areas for growth. He presents the prompt as entertainment and insight, not as a test. That framing matters, because an AI estimate is not a measured score. It reflects your conversation history and the model's tendencies, so it works best as a reflection prompt that surfaces patterns you can check against your own experience.

## Practical takeaways
- Try the prompt with the AI you use most, where the conversation history is richest, rather than a fresh account with no context.
- Always ask the follow-up question about why, and ask for specifics, since the reasoning is the valuable part.
- Pick one weakness or growth area from the feedback and turn it into a concrete practice for the next week, rather than treating the percentile as a verdict.
- Do not use the result as a credential or a comparison point. Treat it as a conversation starter about how you think and work.
